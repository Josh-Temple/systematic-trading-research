# Prior art review — JP225 intraday momentum

Date: 2026-10-04

Market outcomes accessed for this research line: **NO**

## Academic evidence

### Gao, Han, Li, Zhou (2018)

"Market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 394-414.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.009

Core result: the first half-hour market return measured from the previous day's close predicts the last half-hour return. The paper reports stronger effects in high-volatility/high-volume and other state subsets.

Use here: mechanism prior only. We do not copy state filters into the primary v0.1 test.

### Limkriangkrai, Chai, Zheng (2023)

"Market intraday momentum: APAC evidence", Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 80, 102086.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pacfin.2023.102086

Open-access paper: https://researchmgt.monash.edu/ws/files/519509174/494419119_oa.pdf

Relevant facts:

- the study follows the first-half-hour -> last-half-hour framework;
- Japan is represented by Nikko ETF 225 (ticker 1330);
- Japan sample: September 2001-April 2021;
- Japan shows positive predictive evidence for the first half-hour return and for the second-to-last half-hour return;
- the paper also examines volatility/volume states.

Use here: direct Japan-market prior for the mechanism. Instrument, market hours and source differ from OSE futures, so this is not evidence that Nikkei 225 mini has the same effect.

## GitHub implementation prior art

### quantrocket-codeload/first-last

Repository:
https://github.com/quantrocket-codeload/first-last

Observed design:

- computes the early return from prior close through the first half hour;
- adds a second-to-last-half-hour sign confirmation;
- enters for the final half hour;
- includes commissions/slippage;
- notebook explicitly observes that much historical profitability came during the financial crisis;
- a later notebook researches a VIX split and the implementation supports a VIX threshold.

Lesson for this project:

The implementation is useful operational prior art, but the sequence also demonstrates why a replication should not add penultimate-return or VIX filters after observing the target sample. v0.1 therefore freezes **no volatility filter, no VIX threshold and no penultimate-return confirmation**.

### TY-Cheng/n225-open-gap-tail

Repository:
https://github.com/TY-Cheng/n225-open-gap-tail

Relevant design lessons from README and commit history:

- explicit point-in-time cutoffs and source provenance;
- futures contract/calendar handling is treated as a first-class problem;
- source/data schema and experiment outputs are separated;
- the repository labels its current inspected OOS evidence as exploratory rather than calling it a fresh holdout;
- later commits change evaluation/admission design while preserving evidence history rather than silently rewriting old outputs.

Lesson for this project:

Contract mapping, source identity, timestamp semantics and sample-consumption state must be frozen and auditable before outcome use. "OOS" is not synonymous with "fresh holdout" after repeated inspection.

## Alternatives considered and rejected for this next line

### Order imbalance

Prior literature reports strong explanatory power of order/limit-order imbalance for Nikkei 225 futures.

Not selected because the currently qualified DataCube route is transaction-price history, not a qualified order-book/BID-ASK feed. Testing an order-book mechanism on trade-only data would alter the hypothesis.

### BOJ ETF-purchase anticipation

Recent work documents volume/liquidity responses in Nikkei futures around anticipated BOJ ETF intervention, especially during lunch periods.

Not selected as the next directional trading hypothesis because the reported mechanism is primarily a non-price execution/liquidity response, and BOJ ETF-purchase-policy history is a distinct policy-event research problem.

## Design principles extracted

1. Replicate the simplest literature-supported relation before adding state filters.
2. Separate predictability evidence from execution/profitability evidence.
3. Make contract-roll and calendar semantics deterministic before reading prices.
4. Use an actually untouched sample and a second unopened holdout.
5. Preserve negative/null results and stop instead of tuning thresholds on failure.


## Independent audit notes on literature translation — 2026-10-04

Two scope differences are intentionally preserved rather than hidden:

1. The APAC Japan evidence used Nikko ETF 225 (1330) during a period when the TSE cash session ended at 15:00. From 2024-11-05 the TSE cash close moved to 15:30. Therefore v0.1's 15:00-15:30 target is a translation of the literature's **final half-hour concept**, not a replication of the same historical clock interval.
2. The cited APAC paper reports Newey-West corrected regression inference. v0.1 instead freezes a deterministic paired-date bootstrap plus a separate sign-translation bootstrap. This is a project-specific confirmatory rule, not an exact replication of the paper's inference procedure.

The instrument also changes from ETF/cash evidence to OSE Nikkei 225 mini transaction prices. A positive result would therefore support a new futures mechanism test, not reproduce the ETF paper byte-for-byte.
