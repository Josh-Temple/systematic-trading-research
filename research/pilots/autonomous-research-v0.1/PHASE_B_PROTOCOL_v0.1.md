# Autonomous Research Pilot v0.1 — Phase B Protocol

Date frozen: 2026-09-28  
Status: FROZEN_BEFORE_PHASE_B_RESULTS  
Protocol ID: ARP-B-v0.1  
Scientific effect at freeze: NONE  
Market data: FORBIDDEN

## 1. Question

Can an adaptive AI researcher search a bounded declarative strategy space more usefully than non-adaptive random search and a simple deterministic adaptive baseline, while preserving trial accounting and a sealed synthetic final holdout?

This is an engineering benchmark. It does not test market alpha.

## 2. Authorization boundary

Authorized:

- synthetic data generation;
- random-search baseline;
- deterministic adaptive baseline;
- AI adaptive search after a run manifest freezes model/prompt identity;
- one-shot final synthetic holdout evaluation.

Forbidden:

- historical market data;
- DATA-HR-003;
- broker or execution connectivity;
- arbitrary candidate-supplied Python;
- changing this protocol after adaptive results are observed while retaining the same run-family identity.

## 3. Candidate space

Phase B uses a discrete subset of the existing declarative grammar.

Fixed fields:

- position_size = 1.0

Search dimensions:

- source: feature_a, feature_b
- transform:
  - identity with lag = 0
  - lag_diff with lag = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
- operator: gt, lt
- threshold: -1.0, -0.5, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0
- side: long, short

Total strategy fingerprints:

~~~text
2 sources
× 6 transform/lag choices
× 2 operators
× 5 thresholds
× 2 sides
= 240 strategies
~~~

candidate_id and researcher_note are metadata and do not create a new strategy.

Any proposal outside this space is INVALID and still consumes one search-budget slot.

A duplicate strategy fingerprint is DUPLICATE and still consumes one search-budget slot.

## 4. Search budget

Per synthetic world and per search method:

- total submissions: 16
- rounds: 4
- maximum proposals per round: 4
- every submitted candidate consumes budget, including INVALID and DUPLICATE
- no budget extension after results are observed

For the AI researcher, fewer than four proposals in a round are allowed only if the response is malformed or explicitly stops. Unused budget is not reallocated.

## 5. Synthetic worlds

Six worlds are used:

- STABLE × 2 hidden seeds
- WEAKENING × 2 hidden seeds
- BREAK × 2 hidden seeds

Each world has two outcome-bearing hidden splits:

- ADAPTIVE_VALIDATION: 512 rows
- FINAL_SYNTHETIC_HOLDOUT: 512 rows

A separate public DEVELOPMENT example contains schema/example rows only and does not expose the hidden outcome-generating parameters.

### 5.1 Hidden world construction

For each world, the host selects a planted strategy from the 240-strategy space using a hidden runtime seed.

Features are generated as noisy autocorrelated synthetic processes. The second feature is a correlated decoy rather than an independent perfect discriminator.

Outcome generation uses a planted candidate signal plus independent noise.

The actual planted strategy, effect parameters, and seeds are not provided to the researcher before final evaluation.

### 5.2 World archetypes

STABLE:

- same planted relation in adaptive and final splits.

WEAKENING:

- same planted relation in adaptive and final splits;
- final signal effect is weaker than adaptive.

BREAK:

- adaptive split contains a planted relation;
- final split removes or changes that relation according to the frozen generator rule.

BREAK worlds are stress tests for adaptive-to-final degradation. Failure to preserve final performance in BREAK is not automatically an evaluator defect.

## 6. Seed handling

Each world seed is generated by the host.

Before any adaptive evaluation for that world:

- compute SHA-256 commitment of the seed;
- record the commitment in the host ledger/run packet;
- do not reveal the seed to the researcher.

After the final synthetic holdout is evaluated:

- the seed may be revealed in the completed run packet for reproduction.

Changing a world seed after its commitment requires a new world/run identity.

## 7. Adaptive feedback

For each submitted candidate, the researcher may receive only:

- candidate_id;
- validity_status;
- duplicate indicator;
- adaptive objective if VALID;
- coarse rejection reason if INVALID.

Primary adaptive objective:

- mean_return on ADAPTIVE_VALIDATION.

The researcher does not receive:

- raw adaptive outcomes;
- raw final outcomes;
- planted strategy;
- hidden seed;
- final score before search ends;
- evaluator filesystem paths.

Exact adaptive objective values may be returned. This is intentionally a relatively informative Phase B v0.1 feedback condition.

## 8. Final candidate selection

The researcher does not manually choose which candidate reaches final evaluation.

After the 16-submission budget closes, the host selects:

1. highest adaptive mean_return among VALID, non-duplicate submitted strategies;
2. if tied exactly, lexicographically smallest strategy fingerprint.

Only that selected strategy is evaluated on FINAL_SYNTHETIC_HOLDOUT.

The final holdout is evaluated once per method/world run.

A code/runtime failure may be retried only if the candidate, evaluator, dataset identity, and protocol remain unchanged and the failed attempt returned no final metric.

## 9. Baselines

### 9.1 Random search

For each world:

- sample 16 unique strategy fingerprints uniformly without replacement from the 240-strategy space;
- evaluate all 16 on adaptive validation;
- select the final candidate using the common host rule.

To estimate baseline variability cheaply, run 200 random-search repetitions per world.

Random-baseline seeds are separate from hidden world seeds and are recorded in the run packet.

### 9.2 Deterministic adaptive baseline

Use four rounds of four proposals.

Round 1:

- four deterministic seed candidates chosen from a protocol-defined ordering.

Rounds 2–4:

- take the best valid strategy seen so far;
- enumerate one-field neighbors in deterministic field order;
- submit the first four unseen neighbors;
- if fewer than four unseen one-field neighbors remain, fill from the canonical strategy ordering.

One-field changes may modify exactly one of:

- source;
- transform/lag pair;
- operator;
- threshold;
- side.

No stochastic tuning is allowed in this baseline.

## 10. AI researcher

The AI researcher operates in four rounds.

Before the first round, a RUN_MANIFEST must freeze:

- model/provider identity;
- system/research instruction text or immutable hash;
- candidate-space reference;
- protocol commit;
- evaluator commit;
- world IDs;
- search budget;
- feedback schema.

For each round, the researcher receives:

- protocol-visible candidate space;
- prior proposals for that run;
- returned adaptive feedback for those proposals;
- remaining budget.

It returns up to four candidate JSON objects.

The AI may explain its reasoning in researcher_note, but notes do not affect evaluation.

No tools that can read host files, hidden datasets, or repository generator code are permitted in the researcher execution environment.

## 11. Search ledger

Every submission is appended by the host, not the researcher.

Required event fields include:

- run_id;
- world_id;
- method;
- round;
- submission_index;
- candidate_id;
- candidate_hash;
- strategy_fingerprint when available;
- validity_status;
- duplicate relation;
- adaptive metric if returned;
- evaluator identity;
- adaptive dataset identity/hash;
- whether the result influenced later proposals.

At budget close, record:

- selected strategy fingerprint;
- final dataset identity/hash;
- final metric;
- ledger event count;
- last event hash;
- HMAC checkpoint.

A run with a missing or unverifiable ledger/checkpoint is not eligible for interpretation.

## 12. Recorded benchmark outcomes

Per method/world:

- submitted count;
- valid count;
- invalid count;
- duplicate count;
- best adaptive mean_return;
- selected final mean_return;
- adaptive-to-final degradation;
- planted-strategy exact recovery indicator;
- planted-family recovery indicator;
- search-tree/trajectory summary;
- compute/model cost where measurable;
- evaluator/boundary violations.

Across worlds:

- distribution of final mean_return;
- distribution of adaptive-to-final degradation;
- recovery counts by archetype;
- AI result relative to the random-search distribution;
- AI result relative to deterministic adaptive baseline.

No universal superiority claim is permitted from this small pilot.

## 13. Failure semantics

EXECUTION_FAILED:

- host/runtime failure before a valid metric is produced.

INVALID:

- candidate violates frozen grammar or evaluator validity conditions.

DUPLICATE:

- strategy fingerprint already submitted in the same method/world run.

VALID_BUT_POOR:

- valid candidate with low/negative adaptive objective.

FINAL_TESTED:

- host-selected candidate evaluated once on the final synthetic holdout.

These labels are pilot-local and do not modify the repository-wide Knowledge Base schema.

## 14. Stop conditions

Stop the affected run and do not interpret it if:

- researcher obtains hidden seed or outcome data;
- evaluator or protocol changes after adaptive feedback begins;
- trial ledger becomes incomplete;
- budget accounting is ambiguous;
- final holdout is evaluated before budget close;
- final holdout is rerun after a result-driven strategy change;
- a candidate can alter/bypass evaluator computation.

Engineering failures may be repaired only under a new run identity unless the failed attempt returned no adaptive/final result and the frozen scientific inputs remain identical.

## 15. Phase B exit question

Phase B does not ask whether any strategy is profitable in real markets.

It asks whether the autonomous search mechanism is sufficiently auditable and useful on controlled synthetic problems to justify a later Phase C design review.

No market-data access follows automatically from a Phase B success.
